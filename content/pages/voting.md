In American elections, turnout and results can vary sharply from state to state — and within the same state. Arizona is no exception. This module maps <strong>certified election returns</strong> at the precinct level — the smallest unit each county actually runs an election in. Each of the state&apos;s <strong>15 counties</strong> draws and administers its own precincts, so the map below is a mosaic of fifteen separate county systems — and because those lines are redrawn every cycle, each election year is its own map.

## intro

Precincts are where ballots are cast and counted, and where the Secretary of State certifies results. They nest inside — but are not the same as — the districts drawn for Congress and the Legislature: a single precinct can split across districts, and district lines and precinct lines move on different schedules. Reading returns at this resolution shows the actual texture of Arizona&apos;s vote, from dense, lopsided urban precincts to sprawling rural ones.

## mapNote

Pick an election and a contest, then hover or click a precinct for its certified tallies. The default contest is the <strong>headline race</strong> for that year (President in 2024, Governor in 2022) — the same race that drives each precinct&apos;s <strong>two-party Rep</strong> share and party registration splits. Candidate races are colored by two-party Republican share (blue → red); ballot measures by Yes share. The detail panel shows the precinct winner; the distribution chart situates a selected precinct or county against all Arizona precincts.
